# Community Guidelines and Moderation Charter

**Last updated: {{UPDATED}}**

Closet Republic is a place for people who have had to hide. Many of our citizens are still hiding somewhere: at home, at work, in their country. One careless screenshot can undo years of careful safety. These guidelines exist so that nobody gets pushed back into a box, or dragged out of one, because of something that happened here.

They apply to **every space Closet Republic runs or moderates**: this Site, our GitHub repository and discussions, our Reddit community, our forum, any chat or event we host, and private messages between people who met through those spaces. They form part of our [Terms and Conditions](/terms).

## Part 1: Community Guidelines

### 1. The core rule

**You decide your own door; you never decide anyone else's.** Respect every person's right to be as out, or as closed, as they choose, in every space, every time.

### 2. Treat people with care

- Use the name and pronouns people give you. Mistakes happen; correct yourself and move on. Deliberately misgendering or deadnaming someone is harassment.
- Disagree with ideas, not with people's right to exist. Debate about the constitution, the code or the art is welcome. Debate about whether someone's identity is real is not.
- Don't demand that anyone explain, prove or justify their identity, history, body or experience.
- Remember that many members are dealing with stress, danger or grief. Assume good faith first.
- Respect that English is not everyone's first language and that people communicate differently, including neurodivergent and disabled members.

### 3. Anti-harassment

The following are not allowed anywhere in our spaces:

- insults, slurs or abuse targeting anyone's sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, sex characteristics, race, ethnicity, caste, religion, nationality, disability, age, body, class or migration status;
- repeated unwanted contact, including after someone has asked you to stop or has blocked you;
- pile-ons, brigading, or encouraging others to target someone, inside or outside our spaces;
- sexual comments, advances or images that someone hasn't clearly welcomed;
- intimidation, including veiled threats and "jokes" about hurting someone;
- promoting so-called conversion practices, or pressuring anyone to change or hide their identity.

### 4. Safety perimeters

Protect yourself and others by keeping these boundaries:

- **Never ask for or pressure anyone to share** their legal name, location, workplace, school, photos, phone number, or social media accounts.
- **Never pressure anyone to meet offline**, to move to a private channel, or to switch on a camera.
- **Adults and minors:** our forms are for people aged 16 and over. Adults must never seek private, romantic or sexual contact with anyone under 18, or ask anyone their age in a sexual context.
- **Money:** never ask other members for money, loans, gifts or payment details. Need-based help comes only through the official mutual aid process, never from individuals in private messages.
- **Crisis:** if someone says they are in danger, encourage them to contact emergency services or a support line (see Part 3), and tell a moderator. You are not expected to handle a crisis alone.

### 5. Tactical privacy

These are the habits that keep our community safe. Breaking them, even by accident, can put someone in danger, so we enforce them firmly.

- **Never out anyone.** Don't share, hint at or confirm anyone's identity, orientation, gender history, HIV status or membership of Closet Republic outside our spaces, or to anyone who wasn't meant to know.
- **No screenshots of people.** Don't share screenshots, quotes or recordings that show someone's name, username, face or other identifying detail outside the space where it was posted, unless they clearly agree. Blur or remove identifying details if you need to report something.
- **No doxxing.** Never publish or collect anyone's private information, even if you found it publicly elsewhere.
- **Protect your own trail.** Use a pseudonym and a separate email if you need to. Remove location data from photos. Think about who can see your device, your notifications and your browser history.
- **Know the platform.** GitHub, Reddit and forum posts are public and searchable, and may be kept forever. Don't post anything there you wouldn't want someone in your life to find.
- **Keep work and ideas public, and personal details private.** Ideas, feedback and contributions belong in public threads. Personal and safety information belongs only with moderators.

### 6. Content standards

- Keep content relevant to the space it's in.
- Mark discussions of violence, self-harm, abuse or discrimination with a content warning.
- Sexually explicit content is not allowed in our spaces.
- Only share work you made, commissioned or have permission to share, and credit it.
- No spam, advertising, scams, or misleading claims about Closet Republic, including presenting it as a real government or its passport as real identification.
- Satire and lore are welcome, as long as they don't punch down or target real private individuals.

### 7. Zero tolerance

Some things lead to an **immediate, permanent ban**, without warnings, and may be reported to the police or other authorities where the law requires or where someone's safety demands it:

- any child sexual abuse material, or any sexualisation, grooming or exploitation of minors;
- deliberately outing or doxxing someone;
- credible threats of violence, or encouraging someone to harm themselves;
- sharing intimate images or recordings without consent, including deepfakes;
- human trafficking, sextortion or blackmail;
- terrorist or violent extremist content;
- organised attacks on the community, including infiltration to gather members' identities;
- impersonating a moderator, founder or official role to gain someone's trust or information.

## Part 2: Moderation Charter

### 8. Who moderates

Moderation is done by the founders and by volunteer moderators chosen under the constitution. Moderators:

- act on these guidelines and the law, not personal preferences;
- step aside from any case involving themselves, a partner, a close friend, or a conflict;
- keep everything they see in a report confidential;
- never use moderator access to look up members' details for any other reason;
- can be reported to the founders like anyone else.

### 9. How to report

Report anything that breaks these guidelines, or that you believe is illegal, by emailing **{{EMAIL}}** with the subject "Report". You can also use the reporting tools on GitHub, Reddit or the forum, which reach our moderators and the platform.

Please include:

- where it happened (a link if possible);
- what happened and when;
- whether anyone is in immediate danger.

You don't have to give your real name, and you don't need to be the person affected. If you're reporting illegal content, please explain why you think it's illegal. You can send a report anonymously, but we won't be able to update you.

**We keep reporters' identities confidential.** We never tell the person reported who reported them, unless the law requires it.

### 10. What happens next

- We acknowledge reports within 3 days, and act on zero-tolerance and safety reports as fast as we can, usually within 24 hours.
- We look at the content in context, hear from the person reported where it is safe to do so, and decide.
- We tell the reporter what we decided, if they gave us a way to reach them.

### 11. What we can do

Depending on how serious and how deliberate it was, we may:

1. talk to the person, or send a reminder of the guidelines;
2. remove or hide the content;
3. give a formal warning;
4. remove a Role (for example a moderator, lawmaker or contributor role);
5. suspend someone from our spaces for a set time;
6. ban someone permanently;
7. report it to the platform, the police or other authorities, where the law requires it or someone is in danger.

We use the lightest step that keeps people safe, and we look at patterns, not just single incidents.

### 12. Statement of reasons

If we remove your content or restrict you, we tell you, unless that would put someone at risk or the law prevents us. Our message says what we did, which guideline or law was involved, the facts we relied on, whether any automated tools were used (we don't currently use any), and how to appeal.

### 13. Appeals

You can appeal any decision within 6 months by emailing {{EMAIL}} with the subject "Appeal". A different moderator from the one who made the decision reviews it, and we reply with a reasoned decision, usually within 14 days. If we got it wrong, we reverse it.

If you're in the European Union, you can also take a dispute to a certified out-of-court dispute settlement body under the Digital Services Act, or to the courts. In the UK, you can complain to us under the Online Safety Act 2023 and, for privacy issues, to the Information Commissioner's Office. Nothing here limits your right to go to court or a regulator anywhere.

### 14. Authorities and legal requests

We remove content that a court or competent authority lawfully orders us to remove, and we report child sexual abuse material to the appropriate bodies (such as the US National Center for Missing and Exploited Children, the UK Internet Watch Foundation or the Australian eSafety Commissioner) where the law requires.

Because our members include people at risk of persecution, we examine every request for members' data carefully, give only what the law strictly requires, challenge requests that appear unlawful, and tell affected members unless we are legally prevented. We will not cooperate with requests aimed at punishing people for being queer or trans.

### 15. Transparency

Once a year we publish a short report in the Gazette with the number of reports received, actions taken, appeals and their outcomes, and any legal requests, without identifying anyone.

### 16. Changes

We change these guidelines through the community, as the constitution describes, and update the date at the top. Urgent safety changes can take effect straight away and are then put to the community.

## Part 3: Getting help now

If you or someone else is in immediate danger, call your local emergency number (**999** in the UK, **112** in the EU, **911** in the US and Canada, **000** in Australia, **112** in India).

Free, confidential support:

- **UK:** Switchboard LGBT+ helpline, 0800 0119 100; Samaritans, 116 123
- **Ireland and the EU:** Samaritans (Ireland), 116 123; or your national helpline
- **United States:** The Trevor Project, 1-866-488-7386; Trans Lifeline, 877-565-8860; 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline, call or text 988
- **Canada:** Trans Lifeline, 877-330-6366; 9-8-8 Suicide Crisis Helpline, call or text 988
- **Australia:** QLife, 1800 184 527; Lifeline, 13 11 14
- **India:** Tele-MANAS, 14416

Closet Republic is a community, not a crisis service, and our moderators aren't trained counsellors. We will always point you to people who are.
